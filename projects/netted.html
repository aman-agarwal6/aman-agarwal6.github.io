From spreadsheet to requirements

Netted started as an Excel budget workbook. It needed to answer three questions: how much profit has actually been realized, who earned it in a pooled fund, and how much each contributor can withdraw. Before building, I wrote a product and accounting brief based on the workbook:

  • Realized profit = net sale proceeds − the cost basis of the shares actually sold, with each fee deducted exactly once. Sale proceeds are not profit.
  • Pooled funds share profit by contribution weight on the purchase date. Withdrawals reduce retained profit without changing anyone's share, and reinvesting profit is an explicit event.
  • Money is exact: integer cents and exact decimal quantities, never floating-point accounting. Missing cost basis is never guessed; it goes to review instead.
  • Corrections keep history: corrections post an audited reversal and replacement, so the history is preserved.

The product workflow

Users review imported trade records, account for sale proceeds and fees, then inspect realized profit and contribution splits. Shared-fund accounting applies the contribution weights from the purchase date; withdrawals and reinvestments are recorded separately. Budgeting sits alongside these accounting views in the personal-finance workspace.

The public sample presents the workspace with fictional data. It gives reviewers a way to inspect the layout and accounting views without signing in or supplying financial records.

The $76.00 realized gain follows the purchase-date shares: A receives $45.60 and B receives $30.40.

Allocated basis: ($1,000 + $5) × 4 / 10 = $402. Net proceeds: gross proceeds − $2. Realized profit: net proceeds − allocated basis. This demonstrates the project’s fixed purchase-date convention, rather than NAV-based fund accounting. No personal records or market feed are used.

Additional security work: cloud controls

These controls describe the cloud edition in the September 11–13, 2026 review. A separate local edition is under development and deliberately persists device data; the memory-only description below does not apply to that edition.

MFA enforced by the database

A Google password alone doesn't open financial records. Database functions reject any request without a verified identity, a live session and authenticator-level assurance, so the check can't be skipped by calling the API directly.

Imports processed on the device

Brokerage CSV and PDF files are parsed in the browser, and only the trades the user reviews are saved. Imports are capped at 2 MB and 2,000 rows. PDFs are limited to 20 pages, 200,000 characters and a 15-second timeout, and are read as text only, without opening links or attachments.

No local copies of financial data

Financial records aren't written to browser storage or offline caches. Signing out clears private state and rejects late responses, and private responses tell browsers and shared caches not to keep copies.

Isolated from my other apps

Netted has its own hosting project, database and authentication. Its build refuses any other database hostname and rejects secret keys placed in public configuration.

Stage 01 / Local import

What should leave the user’s device?

In the reviewed cloud edition, raw brokerage CSV/PDF content is parsed in the browser. Only reviewed, selected trade records are saved.

Boundary

A compromised browser can still read open content; the local-storage edition has a different persistence model.

Stage 02 / Reviewed records

What does this transaction mean?

Cost basis, fees and realized profit are explicit. Missing cost basis goes to review instead of being guessed; contribution weights are fixed at purchase time.

Boundary

The contribution convention is not a unitized NAV valuation.

Stage 03 / Guarded cloud access

Can this user access these records?

The dated cloud review covers verified identity, a live session, authenticator-level assurance, ownership checks and denied direct table operations.

Boundary

These are the September 11–13 cloud-review controls, not a claim about every edition or every possible attack.

Stage 04 / Journal & recovery

Can restored records be reconciled?

Corrections preserve history through reversals and replacements. A recorded isolated restore matched 506 original journal records, including cent totals and hashes.

Boundary

The journal drill did not restore the complete hosted service or authentication. Operational readiness remains open.

The encrypted recovery copy was restored into an isolated local database, and the full journal (506 original records, 8 corrections and 280 current records) matched exactly. Accounting tests run the real migration in embedded PostgreSQL, covering private reads, blocked direct writes, cross-user references, retries, fractional shares, withdrawals, refunds and statement payments.

Tradeoff and known limits

Contribution weights are fixed at purchase time, not a unitized fund valuation. That convention is easier to explain and audit, but should not be presented as NAV-based fund accounting.

  • The drill restored the financial journal, not the complete hosted service and authentication. A full restore with a measured recovery time is still open.
  • Account isolation is proven with synthetic users; a production test with two real test accounts is outstanding.
  • The assessment is my own, not an independent one. An outside review is one of the four release conditions.
